## Start with the Contract

Before writing a single line of implementation code, define your API contract. Whether you use OpenAPI, GraphQL schemas, or simple TypeScript interfaces, having a clear contract prevents miscommunication between frontend and backend teams.

## Pagination Is Not Optional

Every list endpoint needs pagination from the start. Cursor-based pagination scales better than offset-based for large datasets, and it avoids the classic "page drift" problem when records are inserted or deleted between requests.

## Rate Limiting and Backpressure

Your API will be abused — intentionally or not. Implement rate limiting at the gateway level and design your services to apply backpressure when downstream dependencies slow down. A circuit breaker pattern prevents cascading failures.

## Cache Strategically

Not everything needs to be cached, and not every cache needs to be Redis. HTTP caching headers, CDN-level caching, and in-memory caches each have their place. The key is understanding your data's staleness tolerance.

## Monitor Everything

You can't improve what you can't measure. Instrument your APIs with structured logging, distributed tracing, and real-time alerting. When something breaks at 2 AM, you want dashboards, not guesswork.
